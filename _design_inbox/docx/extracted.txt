Gift  Of Hope\nI\nntroduction\nCharity homes are social institutions whose aim is to offer shelter, food, clothes, education, and proper guidance to children that do not have any other alternative to survive except by seeking for assistance in such organizations as they have no chance to live together with their parents. However, despite their significance and necessity, a lot of charity homes are experiencing various kinds of problems related to their financial status, which becomes poor due to the insufficiency of available resources to cover their daily needs associated with everyday functioning and maintenance.\nThe developers conduct a qualitative research in order to find out what influences people's decisions when participating in charities and donating their money to charity organizations. The results of the survey showed that among the main aspects considered to be really important, transparency stands first. Donors are interested in the transparent presentation of all processes connected with the donation procedures, collection of funds, and so on, as such an attitude will increase the level of their trust towards particular institutions.\nTransparency is now an essential aspect in the management of non-profit entities since the level of trust can greatly affect donor participation and financial contributions. As suggested by Yiming Dai et al. (2025), both financial transparency and performance transparency can greatly influence donation intention as well as the quantity of donations made. \nIn addition, previous donation experiences also affect how individuals respond to transparency practices implemented by nonprofit organizations.\nOther related researches have also shown the significance of transparency in online donation platforms. According to Ignatius N. Hariwibowo et al. (2022), the transparency in online charity fundraising platforms plays a significant role in minimizing the information asymmetry between organizations and their donors. Website quality and credibility were also seen to play an influential role when it comes to increasing donor engagement and trust. Similarly, Nair stated that effective website disclosure increases the sustainability of non-profit organizations since it allows their donors and sponsors to measure the organizationâ€™s performance and credibility.\nTechnological advancements have been instrumental in bringing about automation and monitoring functionalities within the donor management framework. For instance, the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (2024) created an intelligent hundi system, which incorporates computer vision technology and Internet of Things (IoT) to automate donations collection. This system uses machine learning to detect the type of coins and paper bills used, thereby enhancing the process of monitoring. In addition, Anggraini et al. (2024) created an intelligent charity box monitoring system, which relies on IoT and Raspberry Pi technologies. Through this system, donation collection becomes automated and easy to monitor.\nMoreover, G. Venkat Vamsi et al. (2023) designed an efficient coin sorter and counter machine by integrating the mechanical coin sorter with the Arduino microcontroller that helps in categorizing and counting coins accurately. This system automatically counts the total amount gathered and eliminates any manual labor involved in counting, making the process more efficient and accurate.\nTo support these technological advancements, the proposed Gift of Hope system will employ the use of the Smart Donation Box that is meant to make the donation collection and verification process more automatic. For paper bill authentication purposes, the TB74 Bill Acceptor will be used, which is fitted with various inbuilt sensors that will authenticate the bills based on their images, colors, denominations, and other details. The Optical Contact Image Sensor (CIS), which is the main sensor used, scans the bill and its characteristics and compares them with the templates stored in the database. UV sensors authenticate the security codes, while IR sensors scan the infrared security codes in the bills. Furthermore, magnetic sensors will be used to authenticate the magnetic ink used in the bills, while length and width sensors will be used to verify the length and width of the bills to confirm their authenticity.\nThe TB74 Bill Acceptor communicates with the Arduino Uno WiFi through serial communication protocols.As soon as a bill is entered into the system, the machine undergoes an automatic validation process using its built-in sensors If the bill passes all validation checks, classification data are transmitted to the Arduino through the RX communication pin. The Arduino then processes the information, updates the donation total, and reflects the transaction on the monitoring dashboard. Bills that fail verification are automatically rejected by the system, preventing invalid transactions and improving overall collection accuracy.\nTo validate the coins, the Smart Donation Box uses several sensors that combined in ensuring that the coin placed is real. The first sensor used is an inductive sensor, which uses electromagnetic detection to determine the metal type of the coin. The infrared sensor will detect the existence of the coin and use beam-interruption to measure the diameter of the coin. In addition, there is a thickness sensor that determines the coin's thickness and a magnetic sensor that tests the coin's magnetism. As a measure of precision, a weighing load cell may also be integrate for measuring the coin's mass.\nThese sensors are positioned in sequence to create a verification path that will ensure correct verification at the point of collection. The infrared sensor will trigger the system when a coin is inserted into the slot. The inductive and magnetic sensors then determine the coin's composition, while the thickness sensor measures the coin's size. Finally, the weight sensor provides extra verification to increase accuracy and security. Data gathered from these sensors is analyzed using a micro controller like Arduino.\nThe data gathered from donations through the Smart Donation Box system is displayed on the web-based system using various forms of visualizations like charts, reports, and dashboards for tracking. This enables a better tracking of the process of donations. The combination of web-based tracking systems, IoT based donation boxes, and automation of verification processes contributes to improving the processes in the area of charity home donation management.\nAccording to Nimbale et al. (2023), the adoption of digital and IoT based technologies in charitable institutions strengthens monitoring capabilities and improves transparency in donation management. These technological innovations help encourage continuous community participation by providing more reliable and efficient systems for handling charitable contributions. The study covers the design and development of a web-based platform integrated with a Smart Donation Box capable of accepting and validating paper bills and coins through sensor-based technologies and IoT integration. The system includes features such as donation tracking, charts, reports, dashboards, budgeting records, and monitoring of available donation funds to provide organized and transparent management of charitable contributions. The Smart Donation Box utilizes a TB74 Bill Acceptor with Optical Contact Image Sensor (CIS), ultraviolet (UV), infrared (IR), magnetic, and length and width sensors for paper bill validation. For coin validation, the system integrates inductive, infrared, thickness, magnetic, and optional weight sensors to verify the authenticity and physical properties of inserted coins. In addition, the system uses Arduino Uno WiFi and IoT technology to support automated counting and real-time monitoring of donation transactions.\nThe study is limited to the collection, validation, monitoring, budgeting, and management of donations gathered through the Smart Donation Box and web-based platform. The accuracy and performance of the system depend on the calibration and functionality of the sensors, hardware components, and internet connectivity used during operation. Environmental factors such as damaged currency, unstable network connections, lighting conditions, and hardware limitations may affect the overall efficiency and accuracy of the system. Furthermore, the study is limited to selected respondents, testing environments, and available resources during the development and evaluation process. Long-term implementation and large-scale deployment of the system are beyond the scope of the study.
